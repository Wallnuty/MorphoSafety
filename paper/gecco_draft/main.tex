\title[Morphology as a Lever for Safe RL]{Morphology as a Lever for Safe Reinforcement Learning: Constrained Co-Design of Body and Controller}

\author{Author One}
\affiliation{\institution{Institution} \city{City} \country{Country}}
\email{author@example.org}

\renewcommand{\shortauthors}{Anonymous et al.}

\begin{abstract}
Safe reinforcement learning (RL) optimises a controller for a fixed body, yet whether a fast and safe behaviour exists, and whether it can be learned, depends on the body. We study constrained co-design, in which body and controller are optimised together under a safety budget. A Gaussian-mixture distribution over the limb and torso dimensions of a quadruped is evolved with score-function updates, while one morphology-conditioned PPO-Lagrangian controller, with a Lagrange multiplier per mixture component, is trained on a population of bodies simulated side by side on one GPU. On a hazard-field crossing task the co-designed robot is \TODO{55\%} faster than the nominal one at a comparable safety cost. Ranking designs by performance alone selects unsafe bodies when the budget is hard to meet, so the design search needs constraint handling as well. Our MuJoCo Warp stack completes a 2-billion-step co-design run in under 3 hours on one consumer GPU.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Legged robots increasingly have to move quickly through places where some contacts are unacceptable: crops they must not trample, clutter they must not disturb, hazards they must not step on. Safe RL formalises this as a constrained Markov decision process (CMDP): maximise return subject to a bound on expected cost~\cite{altman1999constrained,achiam2017cpo,ray2019benchmarking}. It always optimises the controller of a \emph{fixed} body. Yet the trade-off between safety and performance that a robot can reach belongs to body and controller together~\cite{pfeifer2006body}: legs long enough to clear a hazard turn ``weave around it'' into ``step over it''.

Co-design work optimises the body for reward alone~\cite{sims1994evolving,ha2019reinforcement,schaff2019jointly,gupta2021embodied}, and safe-RL work keeps the body fixed~\cite{ji2023safetygymnasium,kim2024constraints}. We found no work that trains the controller with constrained RL inside a design loop. We ask whether optimising morphology improves the safety--performance trade-off beyond what constrained policy optimisation reaches on the nominal body.

Adding a cost term to the co-design reward is not enough, because the constraint has to hold at two levels. Inside, the budget must hold for every body, not on average over the population the shared controller trains on. Outside, ranking designs by performance assumes the controller already meets the budget; when it does not, the search selects fast, unsafe bodies. The second is the classic constraint-handling problem of evolutionary computation~\cite{deb2000efficient,coello2002theoretical}, with feasibility estimated from a controller that is still learning.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A safe co-design method: an evolved design distribution~\cite{schaff2019jointly} with a morphology-conditioned PPO-Lagrangian controller, one Lagrange multiplier per mixture component, and \TODO{feasibility-first} design ranking.
\item Evidence that morphology is a safety lever: the co-designed quadruped crosses a hazard field \TODO{55\%} faster at comparable cost, and the evolved body changes with the hazards.
\item A failure mode of naive safe co-design: speed-only ranking returns a body \TODO{5$\times$} over budget when the constraint is hard to meet.
\item An open GPU stack for safe RL and safe co-design on MuJoCo Warp, with a different body in every environment lane.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Safe RL.} Constrained RL is usually solved with Lagrangian or primal-dual methods such as PPO-Lagrangian~\cite{ray2019benchmarking,stooke2020responsive}, or with trust-region and switching schemes~\cite{achiam2017cpo,xu2021crpo,sootla2022saute}. Benchmarks include Safety Gym, Safety-Gymnasium and, on accelerators, mjx-safety-gym and CRAX~\cite{ray2019benchmarking,ji2023safetygymnasium,vanderhart_mjxsafetygym,tomilin2026crax}. Constrained legged locomotion~\cite{kim2024constraints,chanesane2024cat} also fixes the body.

\paragraph{Co-design.} Bodies have been evolved since virtual creatures~\cite{sims1994evolving,lipson2000automatic}, and later jointly with RL controllers, through learned design distributions~\cite{ha2019reinforcement,schaff2019jointly}, graph policies~\cite{wang2019nge,yuan2022transform2act} or Darwinian evolution with learning~\cite{gupta2021embodied}. Where safety appears, it is a penalty in the reward~\cite{codesign2025notfree}, the task itself~\cite{yue2025humanoid}, or a design specification~\cite{glamdring2026}.

\paragraph{Constraint handling in evolutionary computation.} Penalties, feasibility rules~\cite{deb2000efficient}, stochastic ranking~\cite{runarsson2000stochastic} and $\varepsilon$-constrained methods~\cite{takahama2006constrained}. Our design search belongs to the estimation-of-distribution / natural-evolution-strategy family~\cite{larranaga2002estimation,wierstra2014natural}; what is new is that feasibility is a noisy estimate that changes as the controller learns.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

A design $\xi \in \Xi = [0.6, 1.4]^7$ scales the lengths and radii of the three leg segments (shared by all four legs) and the torso radius of the Safety Gym ant~\cite{ray2019benchmarking}; the kinematic structure is fixed. For a design $\xi$, the controller solves a CMDP with episode cost $J_C$ and budget $d$. Safe co-design is the bilevel problem
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\max_{\xi \in \Xi}\; v(\pi^*_\xi;\xi) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad J_C(\pi^*_\xi;\xi) \le d,\\
&\pi^*_\xi \in \arg\max_{\pi:\,J_C(\pi;\xi)\le d} J_R(\pi;\xi),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:bilevel}
\end{equation}
where $v$ is the crossing speed. As in Schaff et al.~\cite{schaff2019jointly}, one policy $\pi_\theta(a \mid s, \xi)$, conditioned on the design and trained on bodies drawn from a search distribution $p_\phi(\xi)$, solves the inner problem for the whole population at once. \TODO{One sentence on why speed, not return: return saturates once bodies arrive.}

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\figbox{3.8cm}{Method diagram \TODO{draw}: design distribution $\rightarrow$ population of bodies on GPU lanes $\rightarrow$ PPO-Lagrangian ($\lambda_k$ per component) $\rightarrow$ per-design speed and cost $\rightarrow$ ranking $\rightarrow$ update of $p_\phi$.}
\caption{Constrained co-design.}
\label{fig:method}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Population training on one GPU.} Each design iteration samples a population of designs from $p_\phi$ and gives each its own block of parallel environments. Per-lane physics models differ only in their geometry, inertia and actuator fields, so one compiled step function serves every body without recompilation. The design is appended to the observation.

\paragraph{Inner loop: per-component PPO-Lagrangian.} PPO~\cite{schulman2017proximal} with a separate cost critic is trained on the Lagrangian
\begin{equation}
\min_{\lambda \ge 0}\max_\theta\; \mathbb{E}_{k,\;\xi\sim p_\phi^{k}}\Big[J_R(\pi_\theta;\xi) - \lambda_k\big(J_C(\pi_\theta;\xi) - d\big)\Big],
\end{equation}
with one multiplier $\lambda_k$ per mixture component, so that the budget holds for each subpopulation rather than on average. Multipliers are capped to avoid integral windup.

\paragraph{Outer loop: evolving the design distribution.} Following Schaff et al.~\cite{schaff2019jointly}, $p_\phi$ is a mixture of 8 diagonal Gaussians with frozen uniform weights, updated by a score-function gradient through each sample's own component,
\begin{equation}
\nabla_\phi \approx \tfrac{1}{M}\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{M} u_i\,\nabla_\phi \log p_\phi^{k_i}(\xi_i),
\end{equation}
where $u_i$ are the designs' standardised scores; as in the reference implementation, each design's gradient is clipped to norm 0.5 before averaging. The distribution commits to one design by repeatedly retiring the worst half of its components, each scored on 100 freshly drawn designs under the current policy, and the controller is fine-tuned on the final mode (Algorithm~\ref{alg:codesign}).

\paragraph{Constraint-aware ranking.} Designs are ranked feasibility first~\cite{deb2000efficient}. A design is feasible if its mean episode cost is within the budget and it reaches the goal in at least half of its episodes. Feasible designs rank by speed, designs that arrive but exceed the budget rank by their cost excess, and designs that do not arrive rank last. Arrival is a constraint of higher priority than cost, so a body that never moves, and therefore never pays cost, cannot win. The update uses rank utilities~\cite{wierstra2014natural}. Because a chop is irreversible, a component counts as over budget there only when its cost exceeds the budget by more than two standard errors.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Constrained co-design}
\label{alg:codesign}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State $F \gets$ 8 random families of body designs
\State $\pi \gets$ new controller; \; $\lambda_f \gets$ small safety penalty for each family $f$
\For{each iteration}
  \State $B \gets$ bodies sampled from $F$
  \ForAll{bodies $b \in B$ \textbf{in parallel}}
    \State run $\pi$ on $b$; record its speed, cost and arrivals
  \EndFor
  \State update $\pi$: maximise speed $-\,\lambda_f \cdot$ cost
  \State rank $B$: safe, then unsafe, then not arriving
  \ForAll{families $f \in F$}
    \If{cost of $f$'s bodies $>$ budget}
      \State raise $\lambda_f$
    \Else
      \State lower $\lambda_f$
    \EndIf
    \State move $f$ towards its best-ranked bodies
  \EndFor
  \If{prune step}
    \State remove the worst half of $F$
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return the best body of the last family, $\pi$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

The nominal ant can cross at zero cost, but constrained RL does not find that precise foot placement; a body with more clearance and a longer stride makes the safe crossing easier to learn. Morphology may therefore help safety mainly through learnability rather than feasibility \TODO{test}. Limitations: few seeds; several genes end at the box limit; actuators are rescaled with body size, so size is free; on this task speed and safety pull the same way; and the design space is parametric, because GPU simulators batch one kinematic structure.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Safety is a property of the body as well as of the controller. Co-designing morphology under a safety constraint produced a faster quadruped at comparable cost, and it needs constraint handling at both levels: per-component multipliers in the controller and constraint-aware ranking in the design search.
